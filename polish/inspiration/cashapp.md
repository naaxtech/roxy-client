# Cash App

Category: Peer-to-peer payments and money (consumer fintech)

Why it feels premium (3-5 principles, each one sentence):
- Colour is identity, used as a whole field: the send screen is one flat saturated green surface with black type and a black "Pay" pill (screenshot 2) — no gradient, no glow, just conviction.
- The number is the hero: "$20" is set enormous and bold in the middle of an otherwise empty field, so the one thing that matters is unmistakable.
- A strict three-colour system — green, black, white — with a light warm grey as the only extra ground (screenshot 2's backdrop); money states never need a fourth hue.
- Controls are fully round pills in black or a darker green step of the same hue (Pool, Request, Pay), so hierarchy is fill vs. tint, not decoration.
- Icons sit in plain black circles on white cards (the discount rows, screenshot 4), one glyph each, no tinted squares.

What it does on first run:
- Not shown in the store set.

What it does with empty states:
- Not shown directly; the savings ring at "$400.00 … $100.00 to goal" (screenshot 3) shows progress drawn honestly as a thin ring with the numbers in type.

Its signature interaction (the one thing people screenshot):
- The full-screen green keypad with a giant amount, and the custom Cash Card designs.

Its typography system (families, scale, weights):
- Observed: one grotesk family (Cash Sans-like) — store headlines regular/medium weight at large size with tight leading ("Send money instantly"); amounts bold at ~2.5x the headline size on the keypad; labels ~14px regular; section heads ~16px semibold. Big type is not always bold: headlines are regular, the money is bold.

Its motion language (durations, easing, what moves and what doesn't):
- Not observable in stills. Static cues: the progress ring and the keypad digits are the only "live" elements; cards and lists are static.

What we will NOT take from it:
- Money-app greens or any finance cue in Roxy's palette (green stays semantic: success only).
- The black-and-acid-green "fintech poster" look; Roxy is warm and personal, not transactional.
