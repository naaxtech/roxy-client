# Locket Widget

Category: Close-friends photo sharing (photos land on friends' home-screen widgets)

Why it feels premium (3-5 principles, each one sentence):
- The product lives outside the app: the artifact is a rounded photo tile sitting among a friend's real home-screen icons (screenshots 2 and 5), so the moment is felt on her phone, not inside a feed.
- One warm owned colour (a saturated amber-yellow heart and outline) on near-black; everything else is photographs and system-grey.
- Each feature is named with one plain word and an icon (Send, Rollcall, Chat, Receive) — short, concrete, no feature-speak.
- The photo is always the biggest thing on screen and carries a large radius that matches iOS widgets, so it feels native.
- Replies are a quiet chat thread where the photo sits inline as a bubble (screenshot 4) — the artifact becomes the conversation starter.

What it does on first run:
- Not shown in the store set. The first claim (screenshot 1, "Add your best friends to your Home Screen") makes the first job adding the widget and a few close friends, not filling a profile.

What it does with empty states:
- Not shown in the store screenshots.

Its signature interaction (the one thing people screenshot):
- A friend's photo appearing on your home screen widget; and Rollcall, the weekly photo dump ("Weekly photo dumps with your best friends", screenshot 3).

Its typography system (families, scale, weights):
- Observed: SF-style system grotesk throughout; store headlines bold white ~2 lines, feature names semibold grey with an icon; in-app chat bubbles ~15px regular, names semibold, dates small grey. No display face; scale comes from the photo, not type.

Its motion language (durations, easing, what moves and what doesn't):
- Not observable in stills. Static cues: emoji reactions float over the Rollcall photo bottom-left; the widget simply updates. Nothing in the chrome decorates-moves.

What we will NOT take from it:
- Photos on a home-screen widget by default: for a WLW member a visible widget photo can out her; any Roxy widget must be opt-in and show no names or faces by default.
- Emoji-heavy reaction bars as the main feedback.
