# Headspace

Category: Meditation, sleep and mental health

Why it feels premium (3-5 principles, each one sentence):
- Warmth comes from whole flat fields of saturated colour (orange, yellow, deep blue for sleep, green for focus), each tied to a mode, not from gradients or glows.
- The illustration language is a few soft geometric shapes (a circle with closed-eye smile, a star, a sun) drawn flat in the palette, so it never looks like stock art.
- Each content detail page is one big illustrated cover (top ~35%), a bold title, a one-line meta row ("Course · 3-10 min"), two sentences, then one wide primary button ("▶ Play") at the bottom.
- Choices are made gently: "Choose your teacher" is two calm rows with a radio, "Choose a length" is a bottom sheet with two options — no pressure, no counters.
- The sleep mode switches the whole ground to dark navy with lighter text, so the time of day changes the room, not just a toggle.

What it does on first run:
- Not shown in the store set. The site (headspace-3) leads with one sentence and a single action; the category tiles (Meditate, Sleep, Breathe, Focus, Move, Mental Health, screenshot 2) are the first menu.

What it does with empty states:
- Not shown in the store screenshots.

Its signature interaction (the one thing people screenshot):
- The orange smiling circle character and the colour-field mode tiles.

Its typography system (families, scale, weights):
- Observed: a rounded geometric sans for headlines (bold, sentence case, two-tone words in store art: "Stress less", "Sleep soundly"); in-app titles ~22px bold, meta ~12px with an icon, body ~14px regular dark grey, buttons ~14px semibold white on a blue pill. Headspace's own brand face is Apercu (public brand info, not measured here).

Its motion language (durations, easing, what moves and what doesn't):
- Not observable in stills. Static cues: the cover art is where motion lives (the breathing circle); lists, buttons and text stay still.

What we will NOT take from it:
- The rainbow of mode colours across one screen (six tiles, six hues) — Roxy keeps one owned colour plus semantics.
- The generic blue "Play" button that has nothing to do with the brand palette.
